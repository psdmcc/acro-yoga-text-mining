\begin{abstract}
This paper charts the diachronic evolution of specialized physical postures (\textit{āsana}, \textit{pīṭha}) and bodily fixity (\textit{stambha}) across a digital corpus of 20,529 text nodes spanning the GRETIL and the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (DCS) databases. By executing a high-speed multi-core collocation search, we demonstrate that the terminology of modern postural yoga did not originate within isolated, ascetic forest environments. Instead, our data exposes a hidden historical matrix---the ``Acro-Yoga Complex''---revealing that early postural development was structurally bound to inter-state caravan transit networks, toxicological manipulation (\textit{viṣa}), and clandestine intelligence gathering (\textit{gūḍhapuruṣa}). Our computational mapping tracks a distinct historical shift. In the ancient layers (such as Kautilya's \textit{Arthaśāstra} and Atharvavedic paddhatis), \textit{stambha} and \textit{laṅghana} operated as instruments of state confinement, military paralysis sorcery, and physical border evasion managed by mobile, lower-caste performer guilds (\textit{naṭas}, \textit{laṅghakas}). By the 12th century, courtly texts like Someśvara III's \textit{Mānasollāsa} show the initial high-caste capture of these subaltern physical arts, re-coding active grappling holds (\textit{kūrmāsana}) and pole-athletics (\textit{stambha-śrama}) as elite spectacles. Finally, late-medieval manuals like the \textit{Gheraṇḍasaṃhitā} systematically stripped away these volatile mobile and espionage contexts to isolate bodily stasis (\textit{stambhakarī}) as a static, internal, spiritual technology. We conclude that the lineage of physical yoga represents a long-term process of elite text-composers capturing, sanitizing, and institutionalizing the adaptive survival skills of marginalized traveling acrobats.
\end{abstract}

/\section{Introduction: De-Spiritualizing the Stasis}
The standard historiography of Indian physical culture consistently defaults to an inward, spiritualized paradigm. Postural configurations (\textit{āsana}) and breathing controls (\textit{prāṇāyāma}) are routinely framed as the introspective inventions of stationary forest ascetics working to transcend the material world. Even recent, critical revisions that unearth the martial origins of pole training (\textit{mallakhāmba}) and its modern adaptation into ``pole yoga''---such as McCartney's foundational work (2023) on the \textit{Mānasollāsa} and \textit{Mallapurāṇa}---remain bounded within elite, courtly, or monastic institutions. This paper introduces the \textbf{Contortionist Turn (CT)}: a theoretical and computational framework that strips away this late-medieval theological veneer. 

By running an un-truncated, parallel-core semantic search across 20,529 digital manuscripts, we expose the material underbelly of Indian somatic technology. Our data reveals that the structural vocabulary of physical yoga operated for a millennium as a volatile network of subaltern survival tactics, clandestine statecraft, and inter-state mercantile transit before it was ever spiritualized by monastic text-composers. The body was not a quiet temple for meditation; it was a highly mobile, deceptive instrument used to evade border checkpoints, collect military intelligence, and survive toxic environments along ancient trade routes.

\subsection{The Vedic Bedrock: Seasonal Martial Mobilization and the Frontier Raid}
The historical baseline of the Contortionist Turn is fundamentally anchored within the geopolitical landscape of the earliest Indo-Aryan frontier, as documented in classic historiographical layers like Majumdar’s \textit{The Vedic Age}. Rather than emerging from passive, introspective forest speculation, the semantic root of \textit{yoga} is structurally bound to seasonal cycles of territorial expansion and violent cross-border conflict. Epigraphical and textual analysis of the Kuru-Pañcāla kings demonstrates an explicit alternation model of statecraft: forces systematically un-yoked and yoked their resources, setting out on victorious martial raids in the autumn (\textit{yoga}) and returning to fortified settlements for preservation in the summer (\textit{kṣema}). In this earliest context, \textbf{\textit{yoga}} defined a literal period of time for martial action, consisting of crossing unstable boundaries, capturing cattle, and dominating regional adversaries. This structural deployment on the frontier required intense, adaptive physical capability to navigate hostile topographies populated by adversarial outgroups. Consequently, \textit{yoga} must be defined historically not as an introverted escape from material reality, but as the systematic application of physical effort and tactical agility engineered specifically for the accumulation of territorial property, symbolic power, and material profit (\textit{yoga-kṣema}). This violent, kinetic substrate provided the structural raw material that elite text-composers and monastic lineages later isolated, sanitized, and turned inward over subsequent millennia.

\subsection{The \textit{Ḍomba-Jambhaka} Subsystem: Joint-Crushing, Counter-Surveillance, and the Kautilyan Matrix}

The computational data density of the Acro-Yoga Complex expands significantly when evaluating the deep subaltern lemma sweep for \textit{ḍomba} and \textit{jambhaka} across the parallel processing nodes. Rather than operating as abstract mythological archetypes, these terms chart a precise socio-textual trajectory from violent Vedic physical domination to highly specialized Maurya state counter-surveillance.

In the earliest chronological layer, the root \textit{jambha} fundamentally denotes the jaws, teeth, or crushing physical apparatus. This is vividly preserved in a highly repetitive ritual loop spanning the \textit{Maitrāyaṇīsaṃhitā} (2.8.10) and the \textit{Taittirīyasaṃhitā} (4.4.3), which mandates the physical and metaphysical binding of adversaries:
\begin{quote}
    \textit{te yaṃ dviṣmo yaś ca no dveṣṭi tam eṣāṃ jambhe dadhmaḥ} \cite{MS_2_8} \\
    ``Whomever we hate and whoever hates us, we place them directly into the crushing jaws/locks of these forces.'' \cite{MS_2_8}
\end{quote}
This somatic register establishes that long before the late-medieval invention of an internal, energetic lock (\textit{bandha}) \cite{GherS}, the terminology of binding relied entirely on the violent mechanics of manual joint-crushing and skeletal compression (\textit{niṣpipeṣa}) derived from unrestricted hand-to-hand combat (\textit{niyuddha}).

By the Maurya era, this volatile somatic substrate was systematically secularized and integrated into centralized espionage operations. In a critical diagnostic hit from Kautilya's \textit{Arthaśāstra} (1.12.7313), the state's premier undercover stationary agents (\textit{sattriṇaḥ}) are commanded to master a specific technical curriculum to maintain their performative operational cover along international transit trade lanes:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Sanskrit, caption={Arthaśāstra Espionage Curriculum Configuration}, label={list:arthashastra}]
ye cāpy asaṃbandhino 'vaśyabhartavyāste lakṣaṇam aṅgavidyāṃ
jambhakavidyāṃ māyāgatam āśramadharmaṃ nimittam...
\end{lstlisting}
Here, the mandate pairs the study of structural anatomy (\textit{aṅgavidyā}) directly with \textit{jambhakavidyā}---the subaltern science of metabolic freezing, physical deception, and deceptive camouflage. This provides an airtight textual link for our thesis: the foundational maneuvers of physical immobilization (\textit{stambhana}) were actively classified as clandestine military and intelligence assets centuries before they were spiritualized into monastic configurations.

This tactical curriculum mirrors the material survival mechanics of the \textit{ḍomba} guilds tracked across narrative and legal layers. In the \textit{Kathāsaritsāgara} (6.2.10420), the \textit{ḍomba} is characteristically represented as an agile, apparatus-bound rogue deploying physical levers, ropes (\textit{pāśa}), and peripatetic performance tools (\textit{mṛdaṅga}) to execute quick physical leaps (\textit{laṅghana}) and escape border checkpoints. Orthoprax legal text-composers responded to this mobile agility with severe institutional anxiety. As codified in the \textit{Vaikhānasadharmasūtra} (3.14), Brahminical jurists explicitly compressed these traveling specialists into a single criminalized, kinetic continuum:
\begin{quote}
    \textit{coravṛttād velavo jambhananartanagānakṛtyaḥ} \cite{VaikhDhS} \\
    ``...those who subsist through the path of thieves, utilizing the arts of crushing/paralysis (\textit{jambhana}), rhythmic dancing (\textit{nartana}), and public vocal performance.'' \cite{VaikhDhS}
\end{quote}
The structural lineage of physical yoga is thus inexorably bound to this subaltern underbelly. The \textit{ḍomba-jambhaka} subsystem proves that the mechanical precursors to modern yoga shapes did not emerge from static monastic reflection; they were the rogue survival tactics of low-status contortionists, thieves, and secret field agents navigating an unforgiving geopolitical landscape.

\subsection{The Shamanic Pole: Vaṃśa-Nartin Antiquity and the Economics of Ritual Awe}

The materialist genealogy of the Acro-Yoga Complex achieves definitive historical depth when integrating the ethno-historical tracking of nomadic performer guilds and ritual pole athletics \cite{McCartney2026c}. Deep text layer mapping traces the structural lineage of professional pole play back to the sacrificial matrices of the \textit{Vāajasaneyīsaṃhitā} (30.21), where the physical offering of a bamboo pole-dancer (\textit{vaṃśa-nartin}) is explicitly coupled with an outcaste \textit{caṇḍāla} as a sacred dedication to the open air (\textit{antarikṣāya}) \cite{McCartney2026c}. Rather than operating as secular side-show amusements, these nomadic specialists deployed extreme bodily contortion, vertical column stasis (\textit{stambha-śrama}), and rope-walking as critical instruments of sympathetic magic engineered to avert collective calamity, ensure agricultural crop regeneration, and re-order cosmic chaos on behalf of the sovereign state \cite{McCartney2026c}. 

This subaltern framework unmasks the arbitrary nature of late-medieval boundaries dividing the acrobat, the wrestler, and the yogin \cite{McCartney2026c}. As documented in the 16th-century tantric manual \textit{Sāmrājyalakṣmīpīṭhikā} (107.14--15), the central state apparatus prioritized dangerous, mechanical pole displays on spinning spikes (\textit{atyunnatastambhaprotaśūlāgra}) during the autumnal Navarātra festival to project royal authority and protect territorial prosperity \cite{McCartney2026c}. By executing death-defying acts of physical agility, outcaste performers accumulated massive symbolic capital as living conduits of divine power and masters of bodily stasis \cite{McCartney2026c}. It was precisely this potent, magical capital that late-medieval text-composers systematically captured, turning the volatile, peripatetic survival tactics of criminalized acrobat guilds inward to serve as the sanitized, static, and respectably bourgeois postural catalogs of monastic hatha configurations \cite{Birch2024, McCartney2026c}.


\subsection{The Materialist Triad: Yoga-kṣema and the Guild Network}
The historical architecture of the Contortionist Turn is grounded in a robust, materialist framework driven by the foundational principle of \textbf{\textit{yoga-kṣema}}---the acquisition of unpossessed power and property, and the secure preservation of established wealth. Our digital cross-corpus mapping demonstrates that specialized physical positions (\textit{āsana}) and structural control techniques did not develop out of quiet, un-worldly ascetic isolation. Instead, they emerged within a highly complex, socio-economic triad consisting of \textbf{Merchant Guilds} (\textit{sārthavāhas}), \textbf{Acrobat Guilds} (\textit{naṭas}/\textit{laṅghakas}), and \textbf{Yoga Guilds} (\textit{akhāḍās}). These distinct networks interacted continuously along ancient trade routes and frontiers through overlapping matrices of commerce, ritual entertainment, transit security, and state espionage (\textit{gūḍha-caryā}). Within this frontier infrastructure, traveling acrobat-wrestlers operated as liminal, highly adaptable physical specialists. Viewed as otherworldly performers capable of executing acts of awe, their extreme agility, concentration, and fearlessness allowed them to protect commercial transport lines while simultaneously gathering state intelligence. This interaction enabled internal Buddhist tantric energy clamps (\textbf{\textit{bandhas}}) to systematically evolve into structural, external postures (\textit{āsanas}). Ultimately, the warrior ascetic yoga guilds institutionalized within regional training yards actively adopted these dynamic, contortionist configurations, weaponizing the physical skills of the subaltern to advance their own socio-political power and secure territorial properties (\textit{yoga-kṣema}).

The DCS breaks massive texts down into individual, lemmatized data columns, mapping every word back to its dictionary base form (\textit{lemma}) regardless of how its spelling changes contextually.

\subsection{Diachronic Continuities: The Ethnographic Mapping of the Naṭ and Kabūtari}
The historical trajectory of the subaltern performer guild (\textit{naṭa}) is further validated by modern ethnographic records from the colonial frontier. Twentieth-century registers, such as Russell’s (1916) survey of the Central Provinces, track the direct material descendants of these classical lineages across a highly mobile complex of marginalized outgroups, including the Bādi, Dang-Charha (rope/pole climbers), Bāzigar, and Sapera (snake-charmers). This ethnographic material exposes a precise, continuing connection between advanced bodily contortion and avian mimicry. Specifically, female acrobats within these guilds are labeled as \textbf{\textit{kabūtari}} (pigeons) due to their execution of extreme aerial backbends and tumbling drops that mimic the specialized flight loops of the tumbler or roller pigeon (\textit{Columba gyratrix L.}). This ethnographic data anchors the aerial mechanics of the subaltern acrobat directly to ancient post-Vedic definitions of shamanic flight (\textit{ut-mad}) and monastic classifications of pole-vaulting (\textit{laṅghanakammaṃ}). Furthermore, the close social overlap between the pole-climbing Dang-Charha and the toxicological Sapera shows that vertical column training (\textit{stambha-śrama}) and venom-freezing (\textit{viṣa-stambhana}) operated as part of a single, shared pool of physical technologies maintained by itinerant communities along regional trade routes centuries before they were sanitized into monastic hatha configurations.

\subsection{The Criminalized Column: Pylwan Gopauls, Kolhāti Poles, and the Colonial Surveillance Grid}
The structural link between mobile combative agility and vertical column conditioning is definitively verified by colonial surveillance records tracking the outgroups of the Deccan interior. Nineteenth-century administrative ledgers, such as Gunthorpe’s (1882) \textit{Notes on Criminal Tribes}, document that itinerant communities designated as the \textbf{\textit{Pylwan Gopauls}} (Wrestler Gopals) operated as a nomadic, marginalized population whose primary livelihood depended on a combination of pugilistic wrestling (\textit{pehlavān}) and the execution of high-level gymnastics performed on a long mobile pole (\textit{khām}). This apparatus-bound mobility is reinforced by the \textit{Census of India 1901 (Volume VIII: Berar)}, which traces the etymology of the wandering \textbf{\textit{Kolhāti}} acrobat guild directly to the word \textit{kolah}---the long bamboo pole utilized explicitly for jumping, vaulting, and physical leaping (\textit{laṅghana}). The census registers confirm that these pole-play lineages, alongside parallel sub-castes like the \textit{Bānsberias} (bamboo-performers) and \textit{Jaithis} (\textit{Jyeṣṭhi-mallas}), shared an identical subaltern social stratum characterized by rope-dancing, public entertainment, and systemic social exclusion. This ethnographic reality allows the Contortionist Turn to map an airtight somatic genealogy running from ancient tribal outgroups (\textit{caṇḍālas}) to medieval arena specialists, and finally to modern criminalized performers. The physical technology of the pole (\textit{stambha-śrama}) did not emerge from stationary monastic reflection; it was a mobile, functional asset of traveling guilds packed onto draft animals across the Deccan, which elite text-composers systematically captured, institutionalized, and stripped of its subaltern identity to serve an orthodox theological narrative.

\subsection{The Indigenous Substrate: Kol Cosmologies and Shamanic Pole-Ascent}
The diachronic genealogy of the Acro-Yoga Complex is deeply rooted within an indigenous, Austroasiatic and Dravidian tribal substrate that predates both courtly and monastic institutionalization. Ethnographic and spatial mapping of the Munda-speaking \textbf{Kols} (including the \textit{Santāls}) exposes a foundational somatic environment where vertical column stasis (\textit{stambha}) operated as a shamanic ritual technology rather than a static physical exercise. Within Kol cosmological frameworks, the physical act of scaling high vertical bamboo poles functions as a literal mechanism of ritual ascent to commune with cosmic polarities, specifically the Sun god (\textit{Siṅ Boṅga}) and the Moon goddess (\textit{Ñinda Chando}), alongside more complex Dravidian configurations of the Great Mother Goddess and Sky Father. This indigenous practice establishes the material raw material of pole conditioning (\textit{stambha-śrama}) within a non-Brahminized, forest-dwelling topography. Geographically concentrated across the \textit{Kolha} macro-corridors of the subcontinent, these tribal populations represent the direct historical ancestors of the peripatetic acrobat guilds documented in later colonial tracking registers as the pole-leaping \textbf{\textit{Kolhātis}} and classical \textit{Kollāṭas}. The trajectory remains clear: the somatic mechanics of vertical apparatus athletics were systematically extracted from the ritualized, ecstatic climbing traditions of indigenous Adivasi communities before being captured by regional states for fortification science, and ultimately sanitized into the introspective postural technologies of late-medieval hatha yoga.

This diagnostic intersection demonstrates that the study of the body's vital channels (\textit{marman}) and breath pathways (\textit{prāṇa}) was originally a closely guarded, weaponized tactical technology. Their extreme physical agility was not a spiritual display; it was an occupational asset and a physical mechanism used to survive dangerous environments, target adversarial anatomy, and execute covert operations undetected.

\subsection{The Geopolitical Formula: From Vedic Trampling to Arthaśāstra Utthāna}
The operational transition of \textit{yoga-kṣema} from an epic, pastoral binary into a strategic technology of statecraft is explicitly codified by pairing late-Rigvedic conquest metrics with Kautilyan political science. In the ritual architecture of \textit{Ṛgveda} 7.054.03cd, the binary division of communal activity is strictly partitioned between peace and active warfare (\textit{pāhi kṣema uta yoge}). This framework is inherently aggressive; as detailed in \textit{Ṛgveda} 10.166.05ab, the successful seizure of an adversary’s mobilization capacity and domestic security (\textit{yoga-kṣemaṃ va ādāyāham bhūyāsam}) is structurally validated through visceral, bodily domination and trampling (\textit{uttama ā vo mūrdhānaṃ akramim}). By the period of the classical Maurya state, Kautilya’s \textit{Arthaśāstra} (1.7.01) completely secularizes this alternation model into a precise programmatic mandate for the sovereign, commanding the execution of political goals through relentless, active physical effort (\textit{utthānena yoga-kṣema sādhanam}). Within this Kautilyan framework, \textit{yoga} defines the successful, pragmatic accomplishment of state objectives, while \textit{kṣema} defines the preservation and peaceful enjoyment of that acquired welfare, explicitly acknowledging that such stability is routinely secured through forceful, non-peaceful operations. Specialized physical disciplines (\textit{utthāna}) were thus engineered to service the expansionist requirements of the centralized state long before they were adapted into introverted monastic physical regimes.

\section{Applied Yoga and the Geopolitics of Erasure: From Kautilyan Matrices to Modern Yogaland}

The historical trajectory of the Acro-Yoga Complex achieves its ultimate ideological encapsulation within the transnational consumption-scape of modern ``Yogaland'' \cite{McCartney2021, McCartney2026}. In contemporary state-led branding initiatives and global wellness forums, the concept of ``Applied + Yoga'' is structurally deployed as a Neo-Romantic panacea for environmental degradation, climate crises, and social instability \cite{McCartney2021}. This policy proposal rests upon a pervasive article of faith: that ancient forest-dwelling ascetics operated as nature-loving proto-environmentalist figures who sourced their soteriological insights from peaceful biocentric communion \cite{McCartney2021}. 

A rigorous, diachronic interrogation of primary Sanskrit sources completely disconfirms this disembodied myth \cite{Birch2024, McCartney2021, McCartney2026}. Close reading reveals that long before physical techniques were turned inward as static, spiritualized technologies of self-cultivation, they functioned as strategic instruments of settler-colonial expansion, border fortification, and territorial acquisition (\textit{yoga-kṣema}) at the frontier of the early state \cite{Birch2024, McCartney2021, McCartney2026}.
